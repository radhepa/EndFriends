How a village sits in the End

Islets, bridges and lamps

On the outer islands

A village needs land at its centre and keeps more than 1,000 blocks from the Dragon's island. It sits at the height of the ground around its centre.

Its own islets

Every building stands on an end-stone islet with a rough, tapering underside. Over land the islet merges into the island; past the rim it floats.

Paths become bridges

Paths run from each door to the centre. Over the void they turn into railed bridges with lamps on the rails, and they climb in steps between islets of different heights.

No beds, always lit

Beds explode in the End, so Shulkin sleep in lidded niches and Chorrin in fibre nests. Every walkable space is lit, because Endermen spawn in the dark. Wild chorus never grows across a path.

Build notes

How it is made

  • Buildings are small Python programs drawing into a voxel canvas, checked as they are built: doors need clear space on both sides, chorus needs end stone under it, and no beds are allowed.
  • Villages are planned from those buildings: the centre first, then each building on a ray from it with its door turned inward, its islet, and a path routed to the centre that prefers land and straight runs.
  • In the game a village is one structure: a ground piece that clears old terrain and lays the islets, then the planned tiles (32 by 32 blocks each) placed on top.
  • Find one: /locate structure endexpansion:spire_village, stead_village, crossing or citadel, from the outer End.
  • Not yet: the residents. Every home records where its people will sleep and work, ready for when the Shulkin, Chorrin and the Court are added as mobs.
  • Citadels are walled. Without Enderman Eyes the Court will attack on sight, once the Court exists in the game.
